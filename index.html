F-commerce scale signal

BSS reported estimates of 500,000–1 million entrepreneur-focused Facebook pages and 200,000–250,000 active Facebook-commerce entrepreneurs. Treat these as industry estimates, not a verified census.

The pool is broad, but individual lead quality still needs manual verification.

Strategy: start with apparel and adjacent visual-product niches, then test skincare, custom gifts, home décor and jewelry. Target sellers with observable order volume and operational friction — not every page that simply lacks a website.

Google prospecting toolkit

Search operators help discover prospects; they do not verify activity, ownership or contact accuracy.

City variants to try: Dhaka, Chattogram, Sylhet, Rajshahi, Khulna, Gazipur, Narayanganj, Cumilla, Bogura.

Useful operator patterns

  • site:facebook.com "Niche" "Bangladesh" — public Facebook pages/posts
  • site:facebook.com "Niche" "WhatsApp" — sellers publishing a WhatsApp contact
  • "Niche" "Bangladesh" ("contact" OR "about us") -facebook.com — independent sites
  • site:instagram.com "Niche" "Bangladesh" — Instagram business profiles
  • "Niche" "wholesale" "Bangladesh" — wholesale / distributor leads

Look for business contact details that the business itself published for customer inquiries, and confirm them on the official page before saving.

Lead qualification & scoring

Score observable signals only. A clear refusal is an automatic stop. The same signals appear in the lead form below and are saved to your sheet.

Scoring rubric (max 12)

SignalPoints

Priority bands

  • A 9–12 — personalize and contact soon
  • B 5–8 — research one concrete pain point first
  • C 0–4 — gather evidence or skip
  • Stop Owner declined — status becomes "Do not contact"
A high score means research/contact priority, not guaranteed buying intent.

Minimum qualification checklist

  • Active page / recent posts with visible customer activity
  • Enough SKUs or variants to create discovery complexity
  • Inbox, WhatsApp or comments are a major ordering channel
  • Website missing, broken, slow, confusing or disconnected from orders
  • At least one specific, verifiable pain point to mention
  • Public business contact channel and a path to the decision-maker

Go-to-market sequence
Build depth in one segment before expanding.




1
First 500 leads — sectors 1–5
T-shirt/clothing, beauty/skincare, customized gifts, home décor and jewelry. Research each lead; prioritize quality over raw volume.


2
Next 500 leads — sectors 6–10
Boutique/women's fashion, cosmetics, perfume, saree and Panjabi/kurta.


3
Rest of Tier 1 and Tier 2 — sectors 11–30
Streetwear, kids and baby, gift boxes, personalized products, bags, footwear, home accessories, furniture, haircare, food brands, coffee/tea, kitchen products and educational toys.


4
Test Tiers 3–4 — sectors 31–50
Toys, sports, gadgets, smart home, pets, Islamic products, books, stationery, plants, crafts, travel, cleaning and utility products.


5
Weekly review
Track contact→reply, reply→meeting, meeting→proposal and proposal→win by niche and message angle. Keep investing in segments that show actual conversion.


Core positioning to test
“We help Facebook-first product businesses organize their product catalogue, answer common customer questions faster and connect inquiries to order workflows — with a website and AI support where it genuinely fits.”
Avoid promising guaranteed sales growth. Sell a diagnosed workflow improvement, and confirm feasibility, data access, integrations and human handoff before proposing scope.
